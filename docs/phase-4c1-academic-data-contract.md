## 2. Academic calendar and immutable identity decisions

**C01 — Calendar.** Use opaque UUID primary keys for academic years and terms, with institution/department scope, human labels, term kind, ordinal, timezone and optional dates. A confirmed year has an explicit label and inclusive start/end dates; a confirmed term has inclusive dates contained in that year. Store teaching times separately as local weekly intervals. Proposed timezone is `Asia/Manila`; confirm it institutionally. Actual year labels, first/second-semester ordinals, summer/midyear names, overlaps and exact dates are not inferred from filenames, source-note text or the current date.

**C02 — Legacy term.** Import the prototype into one term with `identity_scope=ccs:first-semester-prototype`, `calendar_status=unresolved`, `year_id=NULL`, `kind=legacy_unknown`, and NULL dates. This is an honest container for existing identities, not a fabricated semester/year. Calendar confirmation may attach verified dates/year without changing that term UUID, scope or existing offering/section UUIDs. Other real terms receive new UUIDs and identity scopes. The unresolved container cannot authorize a new term-aware production run; existing legacy reads remain explicitly legacy-scoped until a separately approved transition.

**C03 — Identity.** Preserve registry faculty UUIDs and integer codes byte-for-byte in meaning. Existing `users.faculty_id -> faculty_identities.id` stays unchanged, including one faculty account per bound faculty and unbound administrator accounts. The new legacy-code relation fills a gap in the existing database: `faculty_identities` currently does not store `faculty_code`. New codes must be unused nonnegative integers and never recycled, including retired codes. Allocation must be serialized transactionally; display labels and names are not PKs. Names are not universally unique identifiers. Verified source aliases preserve matching, but must not silently merge similarly named people.

**C04 — Course/section/offering IDs.** Import current UUIDv5 results from the frozen namespace/key formulas in [Phase 1](api/phase-1-metadata.md#course-offering-section-and-external-identities). A catalog course is distinct from its term/section offering. Preserve course IDs across terms; preserve prototype section and offering IDs for the legacy term. New term section/offering IDs are allocated once under the new immutable term scope, never reused across terms. The existing offering cardinality is one course per section per term; multiple CRNs, cross-listing, shared sections or separate component instructors require institutional decisions and a revised contract before implementation. Course/section identity keys remain fixed; a rename uses explicit aliases/display fields, not UUID recomputation.

**C05 — External instructors.** Keep preassigned external instructor identity/key mapping separate from normal faculty codes and authenticated faculty accounts. The existing public external UUID and internal legacy key both survive. No external instructor is silently added to the normal optimization pool or given an account. Preserve the existing locked external-offering/room declaration and its validators, including the current ICT3A rule; do not infer a new policy from this schema.

## 3. Academic meaning and validation invariants

**C06 — Qualifications, specializations, eligibility.** Qualifications may eventually record verified academic/professional credentials, but the current qualifications CSV contains specialization tags, an auto-qualification flag and provenance, not degrees, degree levels, rank or employment records. Missing degree/rank values remain unknown. Specialization and course-domain relationships are descriptive; they cannot grant teaching eligibility. Each effective permission is an explicit faculty/course decision with retained evidence. Current loading grants eligibility only for rows normalized to `eligible`; absence grants nothing. All 39 inspected eligibility rows are eligible with no duplicate faculty/course pairs. Conflicting, duplicate or novel status imports require review, never an invented deny/grant precedence. Retain unused `auto_qualify_by_specialization` as import provenance, not as authorization logic.

**C07 — Courses and offerings.** Separate the stable catalog course from dataset-versioned title, units, year-level and component requirements; each offered course/section belongs to exactly one term. A domain row with seasonal flags is not an offering and does not supply missing credit/contact hours. Catalog identities may exist without a schedulable version, preserving descriptive metadata in import provenance until verified. Inspection found 37 domain rows and three eligibility rows whose course keys are outside the 19-course offered catalog. Their identities/tags/eligibility must not be dropped or forced into invented offerings: eligibility and domain relationships therefore reference stable catalog identities, while schedulable requirements and offerings reference dataset versions. Preserve the current specialty-track expansion rules in `Functions.py`; do not turn every catalog row into an offering for every section.

**C08 — Components.** Positive lecture/laboratory hours create components; zero hours create no component row/card. Preserve the distinction between credit units and contact minutes. Proposed storage uses exact decimal credit units and integer weekly minutes; reject lossy conversions rather than rounding. Current supported patterns are 120-minute single lectures, 180-minute single lectures or paired 90-minute lectures, and a continuous single 180-minute laboratory. Store the actual permitted day/time template alternatives and their source order, not a Cartesian product of arbitrary days and times. Unsupported hours remain explicitly unsupported and cannot be activated for generation. Lecture and laboratory can use different rooms, while the current subject object has one assigned instructor across its components.

**C09 — Sections and fixed blocks.** Preserve stable section IDs, codes, year levels, construction order and fixed section occupancy. The existing 40 students per section are synthetic runtime objects; they are not an enrollment roster or verified section size. Keep real enrollment count NULL until verified. Consolidated external/general-education course labels may remain external labels on fixed blocks; do not invent fully defined catalog courses merely to satisfy a FK. Parse each source row with the existing day/time rules; quarantine malformed, out-of-grid or missing-section rows. Current loader warnings/partial application must be reconciled explicitly before an import is called equivalent; do not silently repair institutional schedules or discard warned rows.

**C10 — Rooms and availability.** Current rooms have names and `Lecture`/`Laboratory` types, but no IDs, capacities, buildings, features or authoritative availability file. Allocate a stable room UUID once; retain original names and normalized locked-room aliases. Capacity remains NULL/unknown, not zero and not an inferred 40. Store verified capacity/availability later, with source and effective dataset. Import the existing weekly room grid as a clearly identified legacy assumption. Hard room type, non-overlap and locked-room behavior remain unchanged. Storing capacity is not authorization to add a new GA capacity constraint; activation of any new capacity/availability rule requires separately approved compatibility tests. No undocumented lecture/lab interchange is allowed.

**C11 — Workload, release and priority.** Snapshot admin/research/extension release credits, construction seniority/priority, and raw CSV Teaching Load separately. The CSV Teaching Load is source reference; it is not the authoritative teaching target. Preserve `required_teaching_load=max(0,24-admin-research-extension)`; do not invent a CHECK that rejects release sums above 24 when the existing calculation clamps to zero. Missing workload rows currently default releases to zero: retain a visible `legacy_default` provenance state and require institutional review, rather than interpreting them as confirmed zero releases. The model and supported settings/scoring paths have overlapping workload fields; store both raw and effective settings without silently harmonizing them. Current teaching load is per-run state/analysis, not a mutable master balance. A construction priority and the scorer's dashboard `faculty_priority` override/fallback are different sources; retain both and their ordering. Only administrators may alter priority/release/eligibility. Do not introduce new weights, formulas or workload ceilings through CRUD.

**C12 — Preferences.** New academic preference revisions are keyed by `(dataset, faculty UUID)` and mapped through the immutable integer code for legacy responses/scoring. Preference ranks remain course-level, not automatically offering-specific. Preserve all five enable flags independently from stored values, all importance integers 0..5, both style enums, optional time range, ranked courses and ordered blocks. Zero/false/NULL, missing fields, missing record and explicit neutral choices are distinct; retain private raw import payload plus field-presence metadata so defaults do not change old meaning. The JSON is authoritative for actual user preferences; old CSV contributes legacy priority to the current scorer. Do not import old CSV day/time/subject wishes as active dashboard choices. Carry-forward into a different term/dataset is an explicit reviewed draft copy, with eligibility/offerings revalidation and faculty confirmation; never automatic activation.

New preference writes retain the existing strict [validation contract](../backend/services/preference_validation_service.py): supported teaching days, valid HH:MM in 07:30..22:00, paired start/end, unique ranks/day values/block IDs, general-block null subject fields, subject blocks belonging to ranked eligible offered courses, and complete supported component patterns. Current general preference intervals need not be on the GA's 30-minute grid; do not add that restriction to preference writes without separate approval. Preserve opaque block IDs rather than forcing UUIDs. Historical invalid/extra/missing fields are retained privately and flagged, not silently rewritten into a newly validated choice.

**C13 — Versioning and deactivation.** Stable identity rows survive deactivation. Academic dataset revisions are append-only once validated; editing clones a new draft and records lineage. Preference revisions are append-only with a separately updated head. Locking/archiving a term prevents academic/preference writes; reads of authorized historical data remain possible. Current status alone cannot redefine the inputs of an old run. Deactivated faculty/courses/rooms cannot enter a new validated dataset, but old dataset rows and FKs remain. Deactivating an academic profile does not silently delete identities, unbind accounts or change login/session policy; the institution must decide whether a separate account-disable/revocation action is required. Fresh generation must reject a stale active dataset after relevant deactivation until an approved replacement is selected.

## 4. Ownership, API and transaction contract

| Operation | Proposed authorized actor and enforcement |
|---|---|
| Manage terms, catalog, profiles, release credits, rooms, eligibility or imports | Server-verified administrator; academic scope check, CSRF/Origin protection, transaction and audit event |
| View all academic profiles/preferences or import issues | Administrator; avoid credentials and unrestricted private qualification evidence in ordinary metadata DTOs |
| View own profile/eligibility or edit own open-term preferences | Verified faculty account whose existing UUID binding matches the requested faculty; explicit dataset/term; permitted-field allowlist |
| Change own eligibility, release, priority, identity/code, account binding or academic calendar | Denied to faculty, regardless of supplied UI flags/headers |
| Read external instructor metadata | Limited approved display/locked-assignment fields; no inferred normal-faculty ownership or account access |
| Archive/deactivate | Administrator; historical retention, dependent-reference checks and concurrency guard; no cascading academic deletes |
| Anonymous access | No new academic access; retain current authenticated/role-protected boundaries |

Account roles remain the existing `admin`/`faculty`; a term selector or client faculty ID is never proof of permission. New scope checks must fail closed without retroactively claiming current multi-institution authorization exists. API responses must project DTOs, never SQLAlchemy credential/session models.

Current routes and wrappers stay intact: `/api/faculty/`, `/api/faculty/{id}`, `/api/faculty/{id}/eligible-subjects`, `/api/subjects/available`, and `/preferences/{faculty_code}`. Their term-less behavior remains explicitly attached to the prototype path until a reviewed API transition. Do not let a process-global 'active term' silently change those endpoints or a running GA. Proposed additive routes under `/api/academic/terms/{term_id}/datasets/{dataset_id}/...` use UUID faculty/course/offering IDs and explicit revisions; this is a design namespace, not an existing route. New clients must not fall back to a different term on 404/503. Existing frontend consumers in [faculty.ts](../frontend/src/api/faculty.ts), [subjects.ts](../frontend/src/api/subjects.ts) and [preferences.ts](../frontend/src/api/preferences.ts) need explicit adapter/contract review before switching.

Proposed writes require a revision/ETag and compare-and-swap update of the appropriate head in the same transaction as all child rows/audit events. Return 409 or 412 for stale revision, 422 for invalid domain input, 401/403 for authentication/ownership failure, and sanitized 503 for unavailable storage. Do not claim these stale-edit semantics exist in the current JSON API. Use bounded connect/statement/lock timeouts and one transaction per aggregate update; never commit a preference header before its ranks/blocks. Serialize head publication, identity-code allocation and deactivation checks. Cross-row pattern completeness, scoped FKs and immutable validated datasets need explicit PostgreSQL tests, not just Pydantic validation. Run-input capture uses a consistent read transaction to pin dataset, preference revisions and effective settings together.

## 6. Migration, compatibility and rollback gates

1. **Approve calendar and policy decisions first.** Authorize an isolated target separately, confirm identity/source ownership and obtain a protected backup outside Git. Never use a default URL fallback or a populated acceptance database as an empty migration target. No database connection is part of this milestone.
2. **Inventory read-only sources.** Record logical filenames, cryptographic hashes, parser/adapter versions and row counts in a private import batch. Capture registry UUID/code relationships and existing account bindings without exporting credentials/session rows. No academic migration should log raw faculty/qualification/preference data.
3. **Dry-run parse and reconcile.** Build a private staging model with the existing normalization rules; preserve original row/order/presence metadata. Identify unknown IDs, duplicate/conflicting facts, missing releases, unsupported patterns and all fixed-block warnings. User-reviewed decisions must accompany any exclusion; zero unexpected changes is the release condition. Dry run must not write source JSON/CSV.
4. **Additive schema in a separately authorized disposable database.** A future Alembic revision follows `4a_0001`; do not edit or rerun the historical migration as a seed command. Exercise failure/rollback and constraints first with synthetic data. Later institutional import requires independent approval. Each import batch publishes atomically/idempotently from reviewed source hashes; mismatch stops it rather than upserting over existing differing records.
5. **Shadow adapters before writes.** Compare identity maps, all existing metadata DTOs and legacy construction inputs against frozen file-backed fixtures. Verify course/offering/section IDs, ordered lists/sets, templates, qualifications, eligibility, release and preference semantics. Existing API routes and the running GA continue using their legacy inputs during this stage.
6. **Separate activation from schema/import.** Database authority, preference writes, academic CRUD, new terms and GA integration are independent approval gates. Do not dual-write files and DB without a designed atomicity/recovery contract. Rollback before activation leaves legacy readers unchanged; after academic writes, stop writes and reconcile new revisions explicitly rather than dropping tables, overwriting the file store or losing new data.

**GA compatibility is a blocking integration requirement, not an assumed benefit of identical DTOs.** The current [input fingerprint](../backend/services/ga_progress_service.py) hashes names and raw bytes of **all** `backend/data/*.csv` and `*.json`, including otherwise-unused test files. The [engine fingerprint](../backend/services/ga_checkpoint_service.py) hashes GA Python files and selected services plus runtime/package details. Changing these sources, creating a new data-directory export, or redirecting fingerprint logic can invalidate continuation even when database rows look equivalent.

The approved initial 4C.2 boundary should therefore be additive schema/shadow repositories only: no edits to fingerprinted GA/model/service files, no automatic DB-backed GA cutover, and no JSON/CSV export into `backend/data`. A later adapter must preserve exact legacy classes, numeric codes, object graph relationships, constructor defaults, source ordering, eligibility sets, time blocks, external keys, random states, frozen scoring policy and evaluation-call behavior. SQL result order must be explicit and match captured legacy order; `ORDER BY UUID` is not a compatibility strategy. New UUID allocation must not consume Python/NumPy GA RNG.

Separate a database revision hash from the unchanged legacy raw-input/engine fingerprints; they are different provenance claims and must never be substituted or spoofed. Capture dataset and preference revision IDs in a sidecar/application layer only after an approved design; do not insert new attributes into serialized GA objects or change checkpoint schema/engine versions in this milestone. Existing paused runs remain on their original file/runtime compatibility path; saved best results are not resumable populations. Historical scores/unknown inputs stay historical, not recalculated against current terms. Exact continuation under a DB-backed engine remains **UNVERIFIED / NOT APPROVED** until a separate plan proves it or explicitly scopes new runs while retaining the old engine for old runs.

## 10. Confirmed professor requirements: C14 and C15

**C14 — External/GEC commitments before CCS generation.** Other colleges prepare GEC/non-CCS schedules and supply them to CCS. An authorized CCS administrator manually enters them or imports CSV/XLSX into private staging. Every row retains source/issuer provenance, original source-row identity and an explicit draft/tentative/review/confirmation distinction. Manual entry is first-class and requires no fabricated file hash. Preview and review precede confirmation; invalid, duplicate, ambiguous or unrepresentable rows block activation rather than being rounded or silently skipped. Confirmed revision payloads are immutable; corrections/withdrawals create reviewed successor revisions and a new versioned constraint set, retaining the previous version and provenance.

Each relevant student section is a hard resource. A declared applicable faculty/instructor or room must be mapped to its stable resource identity before conflict-free confirmation can be claimed. Names are not resource IDs; unknown is not automatically not-applicable. Enforce section, instructor and room conflicts wherever resources/date windows overlap. Section-only legacy CSV coverage cannot be claimed as full instructor/room protection. Shared-resource mapping across departments and partial-resource confirmation policy require institutional confirmation before activation.

Only a pinned, reviewed confirmed constraint set may enter a future GA input adapter. Tentative data stays visible in review but does not reserve hard calendar blocks. Confirmed external meetings must remain present and byte/semantic-equivalent in accepted results: the GA cannot move, overwrite or erase them. Existing time-free instructor/room preassignment is a different concept and must not be treated as a fixed pre-plotted meeting. New external facts arriving during a run invalidate publication against a stale head; preserve its original run/schedule artifacts and require revalidation/replanning, never modify a checkpoint or silently continue against different constraints.

**C15 — Faculty-requested, Chair-approved schedule revisions.** The GA produces an immutable proposed schedule with an explicit term, department scope, academic-input snapshot and external-constraint-set reference. Faculty see their own assigned schedule and may submit an allowlisted proposal affecting only their assigned CCS meetings. A request does not edit that schedule, preferences, eligibility, workload limits or external commitments. Proposed initial change fields are day/time/room with complete component groups and retained instructor/section/offering identity; faculty swaps, other-faculty edits and external changes require a separately designed workflow.

Every request must be approved or rejected by a currently authorized Department Chair for that department. Existing account roles remain `admin`/`faculty`; a proposed separately assigned, effective-dated department capability supplies Chair authority without assuming an implemented `chair` role. The requester, client flags and ordinary administrator status cannot confer approval power. Proposed default is no self-approval; delegation and Chair requests need institutional confirmation and must fail closed until resolved. Existing route guards remain unchanged during design.

Approval rechecks requester ownership, the Chair's current scoped authority, request/base-schedule versions, the active external constraint head and **all hard constraints across the complete candidate schedule**, including unchanged meetings and applicable external section/faculty/room commitments. No Chair/administrator override permits an invalid schedule. Soft preferences may be reported as advisory impacts but do not become an unreviewed fitness calculation or new hard rule.

Validation evidence is bound to the exact candidate, academic input and external-set hashes and validator version. Submit-time/preview validity is not reusable approval authority. In one bounded transaction, lock/recheck the affected scope/resource heads and request, enforce compare-and-swap, create the new immutable schedule revision, record the one-time decision/audit and advance the selected head. A stale or concurrently changed base requires explicit rebase/resubmission, not automatic merging or blind retries. Failed validation/storage/authority checks save no partial decision or schedule update. Rejection records only the decision/request/audit and leaves all schedule revisions and the selected head unchanged.

The future relational extensions and dependency boundaries are specified in [schema section 9](architecture/phase-4c1-relational-schema.md#9-confirmed-requirement-extensions-2026-10-20). Open policy decisions and proposed milestones/tests are in the workflow design. C14/C15 confirm required outcomes; they do not approve any additional migration or change existing GA equations, operators, RNG, fingerprints, checkpoints, schedules, identities or protected preferences.
